W1 · The problem

Why the obvious comparison is worthless

The naive experiment — take a CLIP-family image tower, take a text retriever, compare them — changes the encoder and the modality at the same time. Any difference is unattributable. Almost every public comparison of this kind is confounded this way.

The confound

SigLIP image vs Jina text differs in: training objective, training data, parameter count, tokenizer, output dimensionality, and input modality. Six variables, one measurement.

The fix we exploit

Dual-tower vision-language models project both towers into a shared space. So the same model can represent a product by its photo or its title, with the query encoder held completely fixed.

The two contrasts this buys us

text-siglip vs text-jinaModality (both read the title)EncoderDoes model choice matter?
Without the same-encoder control, every number in this study would be uninterpretable. It is the single most important design decision in the series.

W1 · Evaluation harness

How relevance is graded

Labels reproduce the production LTR judgement-list conventions exactly, so results are comparable to what the deployed ranker trains against.

Construction pipeline

  • SRLP page-0 clickstream, 90-day window, DSG / web
  • Inverse-propensity weights per rank: τ = 1/(impr@k / searches@k)
  • Sigmoid time decay, normalised per query
  • Relevance 0–4 from smoothed weighted-CTR quantile bins
  • Candidates restricted to web-active products with title + image

Metrics & statistics

  • NDCG, Recall, Precision, MRR at k = 5, 10, 20, 48, 96, 144
  • MAP (no cutoff). Primary metric: NDCG@10
  • Macro (per typical query) and impression-weighted (per typical impression)
  • Paired bootstrap over queries, 2,000 resamples, seed 20260808
  • Second label set (raw CTR) scored as a robustness check

The win is not "more information", and not "bigger model"

Attributes carry less information than titles

Implication: the labels reward category-level matching far more than instance-level ordering. That is a property of the label definition, worth knowing independently of embeddings.

W2 · The defect

Is the image signal even intact when it reaches the encoder?

Standard practice crops to the dominant detected object before encoding. On retail catalogue photography — routinely shot on-model — that rule selects the wrong thing constantly. This is a full census of 19,468 images, not a sample.

Why the taxonomy fails

The baseline detector (facebook/detr-resnet-50) is a fixed classifier over 91 COCO classes. COCO contains no shoe, no jersey, no glove, no bat. Asked to label a soccer cleat, it must pick something — so it picks whatever is nearest in its taxonomy, or picks the human wearing it.

W2 · The method

MGPL — Metadata-Guided Product Localization

Training-free. Derive a product-type phrase from the item's own title and taxonomy, then condition an open-vocabulary detector on that phrase. The catalogue already knows what the object is — we just have to tell the detector.

Pipeline

title + taxonomy → product phrase → [OWLv2] → box → [SigLIP image tower] → 768-d → cosine

Detection and embedding are entirely separate models. The detector never touches the embedding — its only job is deciding which pixels the encoder sees. SigLIP has no spatial output at all, which is why a separate detector is required.

W2 · The negative result

A large, obvious defect — with no measurable payoff

Three-level ablation: uncropped frame → naive largest-area crop → MGPL crop. Same encoder, same queries, same pools. Only the pixels change.

ContrastΔ NDCG@10Relative95% CIp
image+MGPL vs image (uncropped)+0.0068+1.5%[−0.0022, +0.0154]0.144
image+MGPL vs image+naive+0.0038+0.9%[−0.0049, +0.0122]0.391
A visually unambiguous defect affecting a quarter of all inputs produces a downstream effect we cannot distinguish from zero. This is the week's main finding, and it is negative.

Why this is still worth knowing

Dilution

Only a minority of pools contain images whose crops actually differ. For much of the catalogue, the two arms hand SigLIP identical pixels — the ablation cannot measure what it does not vary.

Label ceiling

If the labels reward category-level matching (W1), sharper instance-level visual detail has little to bite on. The metric may be structurally insensitive to this fix.

Cost discipline

MGPL costs 10.2 extra GPU-hours to index for +0.9% at p = 0.391. Treat image preprocessing as a bounded-value investment, not a priority.

Stop asking which modality is better. Ask how differently they fail.

Averages hide structure. The two modalities are not weakly different — they are strongly different in opposite directions on different queries.

W4 · Validity audit

The labels leak the thing we are trying to predict

Three weeks of results rested on one judgement list. Auditing it surfaced a defect worth reporting independently of anything about embeddings.

Residual position correlation

Label setcorr(position, grade)
Production LTR judgement list (as shipped)−0.291
Raw CTR (undebiased control)−0.253
After our fix−0.177

As shipped, the "debiased" labels were barely less position-correlated than raw CTR.

Grade-by-position, before → after

LTR gradeAs shippedFixed
416.021.2
321.918.3
226.319.0
129.323.6

Grades were cleanly monotone in rank. After the fix that monotonicity is broken — grade 4 now sits deeper than grade 3, which is what a working correction should do.

Root cause and fix

The construction divided weighted clicks by weighted impressions: wCTR = Σ(click·τ·decay) / Σ(impression·τ·decay). With τ in the denominator the estimate becomes r / τ̄ — true relevance divided by that product's own mean inverse-propensity — which systematically deflates products shown at deep ranks and re-introduces position bias in the same direction it was meant to remove.

Fixed: τ now applies to clicks only. The sigmoid time decay stays on both sides because it is a recency sample weight, not a propensity correction: wCTR = Σ(click·τ·decay) / Σ(impression·decay). Every number in this deck is recomputed on the corrected labels.

−0.291 → −0.177
residual position correlation — a 39% reduction in leakage
broken
grade-by-position monotonicity — the qualitative signature of the defect is gone
0.601 → 0.423
production NDCG@10 on head queries — 1st place to 6th
−0.177 is not zero, and probably should not be. A good ranker genuinely does place relevant products higher, so some negative correlation is real signal rather than leakage. Remaining contributors: the propensity model is rank-only, and τ is clipped at rank 48, so deeper positions stay under-corrected. production is now reported as a normal system rather than excluded.

W5 · Test-set redesign

The old test set was 300 head queries. That was a problem.

Every result through W4 was measured on the 300 highest-volume terms that survived the LTR filters. Head queries are short, brand-heavy, and well-covered — they are the easy case, and they are not what the long tail looks like.

Before — volume-ranked

  • Candidate pool = top 6,000 terms by impression count
  • Final selection = ORDER BY impressions DESC LIMIT 300
  • Head-biased twice. Tail terms were cut before any quality check ran
  • 300 queries · 19,468 products

After — stratified, no volume cap

  • Pool = top-volume terms ∪ random sample of low-volume terms
  • No final volume cap — every term passing LTR eligibility is kept
  • Each query tagged head / torso / tail by volume percentile within the evaluable set
  • 6,537 queries · 80,848 products

Resulting strata

324
Head — top 5% by volume percentile
1,635
Torso — next 25%
4,573
Tail — remaining 70%, the long tail
Eligibility, not popularity, is now the inclusion rule. A query is in the test set if it has a gradeable candidate pool (10–240 candidates, ≥2 distinct relevance levels) — regardless of how much traffic it gets.

W6 · Fusion sweep

If we fuse image with text — which text, and combined how?

Target modality held fixed at SigLIP image similarity. Sweep 6 text representations × 3 fusion methods = 18 combinations, all on cached embeddings.

Three fusion methods

MethodCombines
Mean cosine0.5·(cos_img + cos_txt) — raw, unnormalised
RRF (k=60)1/(60+rank_img) + 1/(60+rank_txt) — rank-based
Z-score average0.5·(z(cos_img) + z(cos_txt)) — current production

Why mean-cosine is in the sweep

It is the control that shows what goes wrong without scale correction. SigLIP and Jina similarities are dot products in unrelated, differently-scaled spaces — a raw average silently lets whichever system has larger score magnitude dominate, regardless of quality.

RRF sidesteps the scale problem entirely by fusing on rank rather than score — no normalisation assumption at all.

Six text representations swept

text-sigliptext-jinatext-jina-small attr-siglipattr-jinaattr-jina-small

One combination — (text-jina, z-score average) — is exactly the pre-existing fusion system, so it is reused rather than recomputed. The other 17 are new.

Recommendations

What to do next

Do now

  • Do not adopt attr-siglip. Its advantage was a tie-ordering artefact; corrected, it ranks last among real systems.
  • Keep the two harness fixes. Pool shuffling before ranking, and click-only IPW in the judgement list. Both are one-line changes that altered headline conclusions.
  • Port the IPW fix upstream. The same τ-in-the-denominator bug is in the production judgement-list pipeline, where it affects ranker training, not just our evaluation.
  • Adopt two standing diagnostics: corr(position, grade) on any click-derived label set, and per-pool tie fraction for any new representation.

Do not invest further (yet)

  • Image preprocessing / cropping. Real defect, no measurable payoff, +10.2 GPU-h. Revisit only if the label ceiling is lifted.
  • Modality routing. Two natural routers land at and below chance. This is a research problem, not a sprint item.
  • Larger text encoders. 2.8× capacity buys nothing on titles; the smallest model wins on attributes.

Open questions worth a next cycle

Label ceiling

Attributes win despite being unable to separate within a category. Do the labels reward anything beyond category matching? If not, the metric caps what any embedding can show.

Tail-first evaluation

Now that 4,573 tail queries are measurable, re-run the W2 crop ablation and W3 routing analysis on tail only — the dilution argument predicts larger effects there.

Learned combination

RRF is a fixed, untrained rule and it beat both tuned-looking alternatives. A learned per-query combiner is the natural next step toward the 70% of oracle headroom still unclaimed.
